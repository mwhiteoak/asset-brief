THE STRATEGIC READ

Today's flow says two different things about the same book: income-producing retail is being marked up (GPT's retail WACR held at 5.41% with a +$28.3m positive revaluation) while office cap rates are still drifting out at the non-prime end (Growthpoint out to 7.14%). Debt is the quiet win — Region Group refinanced more than $1bn at improved margins to a 4.5% weighted average cost of debt, fully hedged, and GPT sits at 5.0%: if your facilities mature inside 24 months, the market is currently paying you to move early. Against that, a fully leased inner-Brisbane large-format precinct just traded at 4.63% — below almost anybody's cost of debt — which tells you the private bid for tight convenience/LFR stock is a selling opportunity, not a buying one. Net call: refinance, be a net seller of sub-5% retail into private money, and hold Brisbane office where Knight Frank has no new completions until 2027 refurbs and effectively nothing until 2030-32.

THE BIG ONE

GPT Group (GPT) posted interim FFO of $338.8m (17.7 cps, +5.0%) on $41.6b of AUM, with NTA $5.61, portfolio occupancy 97.6% and like-for-like NPI growth of 5.8%. The valuation detail is the part that matters for carrying values: retail WACR 5.41% with +$28.3m of uplift and 99.8% occupancy, office WACR 6.26% at 92.1% occupancy with LFL NPI +8.0%, logistics WACR 5.61%. Funding was equally telling — the GPT Wholesale Shopping Centre Fund raised $697m of oversubscribed equity and closed a $700m Asian term loan, with ~$1.7b of gross transactions and net gearing 31.5%.

What it means: wholesale money is oversubscribing for shopping centres and the valuers are following — if your retail is still marked wider than 5.5% on comparable quality, ask your valuer to justify it.
